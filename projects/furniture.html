Furniture Sales Performance Dashboard

A furniture retailer in Greater Jakarta needs a clear view of its 2025 performance: where revenue comes from, which products drive it, and whether discounts work.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • Seaborn
  • SciPy
  • Looker Studio
Open live dashboard Open notebook GitHub repository
Data
10,000 orders
Period
Jan – Dec 2025
Source
KarirNex bootcamp dataset

Objectives

  • Analyze overall sales performance.
  • Examine sales distribution by city.
  • Understand the product category mix.
  • Evaluate whether discounts are effective.

Method

  • Cleaning: date conversion, duplicate and missing-value checks, and trimming spaces in city names.
  • Revenue basis: only completed orders count as revenue. Cancelled and refunded orders feed the success and loss rate metrics.
  • Promo analysis: a comparison of discounted and non-discounted orders using Welch's t-test.

Headline metrics

Rp47.48Bcompleted revenue from 8,972 orders
Rp5.29Maverage order value
89.7%success rate

Findings and recommendations

  1. Revenue is evenly spread across cities. Each city accounts for 9.1% to 10.7% of revenue. Recommendation: focus growth on lower-revenue cities such as Bekasi and Tangerang.
  2. Three categories dominate. Bedroom (36.9%), Living Room (26.1%), and Kitchen (19.8%) together make up about 82.8% of revenue. Recommendation: test product bundles to lift the smaller categories.
  3. Order failures are costly. 519 cancelled and 509 refunded orders were worth about Rp5.55B, and loss rates were highest in Bekasi (12.3%). Recommendation: investigate product quality, delivery, and stock availability.
  4. The 10% discount did not raise quantity. Average quantity was 2.04 for promo orders vs. 1.99 for non-promo (p ≈ 0.33, not significant), and promo order value was about 12% lower. Recommendation: run controlled tests at several discount levels before expanding promotions.

Limitations

  • Only one year of data, so seasonal patterns are not yet confirmed.
  • Only 318 promo orders at a single discount level, so discount conclusions are indicative.
  • No data on cancellation reasons or product costs, so profit could not be analyzed.
