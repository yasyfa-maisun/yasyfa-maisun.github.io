Walmart Superstore Sales Performance Dashboard

Where do sales and profit come from? An end-to-end look at sales performance, profit margins, and geographic distribution for a retail superstore.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • Matplotlib
  • Seaborn
  • Looker Studio
Open live dashboard Open notebook GitHub repository
Data
Walmart Superstore dataset
Customers
686 unique customers
Tools
Python, Looker Studio

Dataset

The Walmart Superstore dataset, with order-level fields including order ID, order date, customer name, city, state, category, sales, profit, and quantity.

Method

  • Data cleaning: converted date fields to datetime, checked for duplicates, and handled missing values.
  • Customer count: counted distinct customers to avoid double-counting repeat buyers, giving 686 unique customers.
  • Calculated fields: total sales and profit, the top 10 categories, and the top 5 cities by sales.
  • Geographic breakdown: sales and profitability by state.

Headline metrics

$725.46Ktotal sales
$108.42Ktotal profit
~15%profit margin
686unique customers

Findings and recommendations

  1. Sales are concentrated in California, which contributes about $458K of the $725.46K total and 72.5% of profit among the top three states. Recommendation: expand strategies to states such as Washington ($139K in sales).
  2. Sales peak at year-end. Monthly sales peak around September to December, at roughly $28K–$33K, and Q4 accounts for 36.5% of total profit. Recommendation: focus promotions and inventory planning on late Q3 and early Q4.
  3. Volume leaders are not the profit leaders. Chairs ($102K) and Phones ($99K) lead in sales, while Copiers generate the highest profit share among the top three categories (37.2%). Recommendation: keep Chairs and Phones steady while promoting Copiers.
  4. Repeat customers support profitability. With a margin of about 15% across 686 customers, loyalty programs can help protect revenue from repeat purchases.
